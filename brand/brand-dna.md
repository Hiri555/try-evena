## 1. What the brand is

**PHOENIX VOLETS** — "La référence de la sécurité en Guinée."

Not a shutter vendor. A **security contractor** that happens to sell shutters. The distinction drives every ad: nobody emotionally wants a roller shutter, they want to stop lying awake at 4 a.m.

**Baseline:** PROTÉGER • SÉCURISER • VALORISER
**Market:** Guinea (Conakry first), West/Central African expansion
**Model:** Importation maîtrisée → montage local → formation & transfert de compétences → développement durable

## 3. Positioning

**For** homeowners, diaspora builders and shop owners in Guinea
**who** have watched cheap imported metalwork fail — rusted, bent, or cut open —
**PHOENIX VOLETS is** the security installer whose product is CE-certified, French-origin and assembled locally
**because** it is engineered for tropical constraint (humidity, salt air, heat, délestage) and backed by after-sales service with parts actually in stock in Conakry.

**The real competitor is not another brand.** It is:
1. The cheap uncertified import that rusts or gets cut open.
2. The night guard (vigile) — a recurring cost that sleeps.
3. Doing nothing until after the break-in.

Every strong ad attacks one of those three, not a named rival.

## 5. What we can NEVER say (Meta ads compliance + brand risk)

### Hard Meta policy blocks
1. **No fear-mongering imagery of real crime in progress** — no depictions of an actual assault, a person being attacked, blood, or a victim in distress. Meta rejects shocking/sensational content. Tension is allowed; trauma is not.
2. **No "you" targeting that implies a personal attribute or circumstance** — never "Vous avez été cambriolé ?", "Votre quartier est dangereux", "Vous habitez à Cosa ?". Meta's personal-attributes policy forbids implying you know the viewer's situation. Write about the category ("Un cambriolage sur deux commence par la tôle"), not about them.
3. **No naming or implying a specific neighbourhood as dangerous** — "Lambanyi n'est plus sûr" is both a policy risk and a defamation/PR risk. Neighbourhoods may only appear as *service coverage* ("Nous intervenons à Kipé, Nongo, Kaloum"), never as threat labels.
4. **No weapons, no guns, no machetes** in creative. Instant rejection.
5. **No counterfeit/competitor denigration by name** — never "mieux que [marque]", never a named Chinese brand. Attack the category ("la tôle non certifiée"), never a company.
6. **No absolute security guarantees** — never "inviolable", "100 % sécurisé", "impossible à forcer", "risque zéro". Legally indefensible and a Meta misleading-claims risk. Use "renforcée", "dissuasion", "freine", "résiste".
7. **No before/after of a burgled home** presented as a customer's real loss without written consent.
8. **No fake urgency** — no countdown that resets, no "dernier jour" that is not the last day.
9. **No unverified statistics.** Never cite a crime rate, a police figure or a "X % des cambriolages" number without a public, citable source. If a stat is used on-image it must carry its source line.
10. **No images of real police, gendarmerie, or military** and no official emblems of the Republic of Guinea.

### Brand-level nevers
11. Never mock or shame the customer for their current setup ("votre vieux cadenas est ridicule"). The buyer is prudent, not stupid.
12. Never promise what the SAV cannot hold — no "intervention en 1h" unless staffed for it.
13. Never show a product render that misrepresents what is actually installed (no European chalet, no snow, no Haussmann façade).
14. Never use imagery that reads as not-Guinea — no all-white casting, no European suburb. Creative must be visibly West African.
15. Never present alarms/CCTV as a replacement for physical blocking — the brand's own logic is layered.

## 6. Visual identity

### Colours (exact, from brand board)
| Token | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Navy / Bleu nuit | `#0D1B2A` | Dominant background, authority, night |
| Or / Gold | `#D4AF37` | Accent, logo, headline emphasis, CTA |
| Gris | `#6B7280` | Secondary text, metal, technical detail |
| Blanc | `#FFFFFF` | Body text on navy, clean panels |

**Ratio discipline:** navy ~70 %, white ~20 %, gold ~10 %. Gold is a *highlight*, never a fill — the moment gold becomes a background the brand reads cheap.

### Logo
Gold phoenix inside a shield, navy ground, flanked by a shutter and a door motif, wifi/connectivity arc above. Wordmark "PHOENIX" navy + "VOLETS" gold.

### Art direction rules
- **Real West African context.** Conakry villas, boutique façades, courtyard walls, tropical light, laterite dust, palm shadow. Never a European streetscape.
- **Light:** either hard tropical daylight with deep shadow, or night-blue with a single warm interior light. The night-blue frame is the brand's signature register.
- **Texture:** honest materials — brushed aluminium, powder-coat, concrete, red earth. No glossy 3D-render plastic.
- **Typography:** heavy geometric/grotesque sans, uppercase for headlines, generous letter-spacing on the baseline. Numbers oversized when a figure carries the ad.
- **Composition:** one idea per frame. Thumb-stopping at 4 % screen size or it fails.
- **Anti-pattern (explicitly rejected by client):** the "capability board" — a grid of four product photos with icon rows and a feature list. Zero tension, zero hook. Never ship that layout as an ad.

## 7. Offer architecture

- Primary conversion: **devis gratuit / visite technique** (lead-gen, WhatsApp or form)
- Secondary: showroom visit, catalogue
- Channel reality: WhatsApp is the dominant commercial channel in Guinea — CTA should normally route to WhatsApp, not a long form.
- **[À VÉRIFIER]**: phone number, WhatsApp number, showroom address, opening hours.
